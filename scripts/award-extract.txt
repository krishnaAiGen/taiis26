TAIIS 2026 International Excellence and Impact Awards
Celebrating Excellence in Research, Education, Industry, Entrepreneurship and Responsible Innovation
Call for Nominations Now Open
The Organizing Committee of the International Conference on Trustworthy AI and Intelligent IoT Systems (TAIIS 2026) invites nominations for the TAIIS 2026 International Excellence and Impact Awards.
The awards recognise researchers, academicians, educators, industry professionals, innovators, entrepreneurs, start-ups, research teams, and institutions that have made meaningful and verifiable contributions to artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, intelligent IoT systems, computing, education, entrepreneurship, and responsible technological development.
The awards will be presented during TAIIS 2026, to be held from December 3–5, 2026, at Asia University, Taichung, Taiwan.

Who Can Be Nominated?
Nominations may be submitted for:
Researchers and academicians;
Teachers and academic leaders;
Doctoral and early-career researchers;
Engineers and technology professionals;
Industry leaders and professional teams;
Entrepreneurs and start-up founders;
Registered start-ups and technology companies;
Research laboratories and innovation centres;
Universities and higher education institutions;
Public-sector and non-profit organisations;
International and academia–industry partnerships.
Nominations are open internationally. The nominee’s contribution should be relevant to research, education, technology, innovation, entrepreneurship, or societal development.

Who Can Submit a Nomination?
A nomination may be submitted through:
Self-nomination;
Nomination by an academic or professional peer;
Nomination by a university, department, or research centre;
Nomination by an employer or industry organisation;
Nomination by a professional association;
Recommendation by a TAIIS committee member or invited expert.
A third-party nominator must obtain the nominee’s consent before submitting personal information or supporting documentation.

Nomination Requirements
Each nomination must include:
Name, designation, affiliation, country, and contact information of the nominee;
Name and contact information of the nominator, where applicable;
Selection of one appropriate award category;
A professional biography of up to 200 words;
An award citation of approximately 500–750 words;
An impact statement of up to 300 words;
A list of up to five principal achievements;
Verifiable evidence supporting the claimed contributions;
A curriculum vitae or organisational profile of no more than four pages;
One supporting or endorsement letter, where applicable;
Relevant website, publication, patent, product, or project links;
A declaration confirming the accuracy and authenticity of the submitted information.
Supporting evidence may include:
Publications and citation information;
Patents or intellectual property;
Research grants and completed projects;
Products, platforms, prototypes, or software;
Technology deployment or adoption records;
Teaching, supervision, or mentorship outcomes;
Start-up registration and business information;
User, customer, or beneficiary impact;
Standards, policies, or professional contributions;
Institutional reports or authenticated media coverage.
General promotional statements without verifiable evidence may not be considered sufficient.

Eligibility of Young Researchers
Candidates for the Best Young Researcher Award should normally be within ten years of receiving their highest research degree as of the nomination deadline.
Documented career interruptions—including parental leave, long-term illness, caregiving responsibilities, compulsory national service, or other exceptional circumstances—may be considered when determining eligibility.
The evaluation will focus on research independence, originality, quality, demonstrated impact, and future potential rather than age alone.

Evaluation Process
Stage 1: Eligibility and Completeness Check
The Awards committee will verify the selected category, eligibility of the nominee, completeness of the submission, and availability of supporting evidence.
Stage 2: Conflict-of-Interest Review
Nominations will be assigned to evaluators who do not have a direct supervisory, institutional, financial, or personal conflict with the nominee.
Stage 3: Independent Expert Evaluation
Each eligible nomination will normally be assessed independently by at least two experts using the published evaluation framework.
Stage 4: Evidence Verification
The committee may verify publications, patents, affiliations, start-up registrations, products, deployments, partnerships, and other important claims. Additional clarification may be requested.
Stage 5: Committee Deliberation
Shortlisted nominations will be considered by the Awards Committee, taking into account the expert evaluations and category-specific requirements.
Stage 6: Final Approval
The final recommendations will be reviewed and approved by the authorised TAIIS 2026 Awards Committee.

The committee may adjust the emphasis according to the category. For example:
Academic awards will emphasise research, education, mentorship, and scholarly leadership;
Industry awards will emphasise implementation, adoption, reliability, and measurable value;
Start-up awards will emphasise innovation, market validation, scalability, governance, and growth potential;
Global awards will emphasise international reach, cross-border collaboration, inclusiveness, and societal impact;
Institutional awards will emphasise strategy, continuity, ecosystem development, and verified organisational outcomes.

Integrity and Transparency
Nomination and evaluation will be based on merit and verifiable evidence.
Submission of a nomination does not guarantee selection.
Evaluators must disclose relevant conflicts of interest.
False, misleading, plagiarised, or fabricated information may result in disqualification.
The committee may contact the nominee’s institution or nominated referees to verify important claims.
Confidential nomination material will be used only for evaluation and administrative purposes.
Information about selected recipients will be published only after confirmation and consent.
Normally, an individual or organisation will receive no more than one nomination-based award in the same edition.
The Awards Committee may withhold an award when no nomination meets the required standard.
Decisions of the Awards Committee will be final.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is self-nomination permitted?
Yes. Self-nomination, peer nomination, and institutional nomination are permitted.
Is submitting a conference paper compulsory?
No. These nomination-based awards are independent of conference paper submission and publication.
Can one person be nominated for multiple categories?
A nominee may be proposed for a maximum of two categories. However, the committee will normally confer only one nomination-based award on the same recipient.
Will every announced category have a winner?
Not necessarily. An award will be presented only when at least one nomination meets the required quality standard.
Is physical attendance compulsory?
Physical participation is encouraged but is not a condition of selection. Online recognition may be approved for recipients unable to travel.
Will nomination materials be published?
Nomination documents will not be publicly released. For selected recipients, only the approved biography, photograph, award citation, and relevant public links may be published.
Are awards connected with Springer publication?
No. These awards are independently administered by the TAIIS 2026 Organizing Committee. They have no influence on Springer Nature’s editorial, quality-control, or publication decisions.
